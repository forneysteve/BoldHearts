Introduction
What This Report Is

This report summarizes the results of the Leadership Health Assessment completed by your pastoral and elder team. Ten leaders participated. All responses are anonymous — individual scores are not identified by name. What follows is an honest picture of your leadership culture as your team actually experiences it.

This is not an evaluation. It is a mirror. The goal is not to assign grades but to give your team something honest to work with — a starting point for conversations that could significantly strengthen the culture you lead from.

The assessment measured ten dimensions of leadership health across two categories: relational and personal health (how leaders experience the team culture), and leadership-specific health (whether leaders have what they need to sustain their role).

Section 2 — Key Findings
What the Scores Are Telling You

Raw scores tell part of the story. What follows is an honest interpretation of the patterns — what this data reveals about the culture your leaders are actually experiencing.

⚠ Sustainability is a critical concern
At 42%, Sustainability is your lowest score — and the distribution makes it urgent. Eight of ten leaders scored below 50%. This is not a personal struggle for a few individuals. It is a systemic pattern. Your leaders are serving at a pace that is not sustainable, and most do not feel permission to say so. If unaddressed, this will produce burnout, quiet quitting, and eventual departure among your most capable people.
⚠ Trust & Safety and Being Known are dangerously low together
Trust & Safety at 49% and Being Known at 48% together tell a specific story: your leaders are not in a culture where vulnerability is safe or normal. They work well together, but they don't go deep with each other. Difficult conversations may happen, but honest ones — about doubt, struggle, failure, or disagreement — largely don't. This limits the team's capacity for genuine trust and sets a ceiling on everything else.
⚠ Having a Voice scores low — 51%
Leaders feel they can speak, but not fully. The gap between what is thought and what is said is real. This may show up as deference to authority, avoidance of conflict, or a quiet sense that some things simply aren't safe to raise. A culture where leaders can't fully use their voice will over time select for compliance over wisdom — and lose its most thoughtful people.
⚡ High Calling + High Empowerment + Low Sustainability = Burnout Engine
This is the most important pattern in your data. Leaders feel called (75%), empowered (71%), and clear on mission — and they are running on empty (42%). This combination is the classic profile of a high-functioning ministry team heading toward crisis. The mission won't slow them down. The calling won't either. Without intentional structural change, this predicts breakdown within 12–18 months.
✓ Calling and Empowerment are genuine strengths
Your two highest scores — Calling at 75% and Empowerment at 71% — are the foundation everything else is built on. Leaders believe they're doing what they're made for, and they feel trusted to lead rather than managed. This is rare and significant. These scores represent real capital. The goal is to protect them by building the relational and personal health that makes them sustainable long-term.
✓ Belonging is a real asset
At 67%, Belonging is the strongest relational score. Leaders feel genuinely wanted on this team — not just useful. This warmth is real and provides the relational foundation for going deeper. It means the goodwill exists. What's missing is the safety and permission to use it for honest conversation.
Section 3 — Culture Patterns
The Shape of Your Culture

Score combinations reveal more than individual numbers. Here are the dominant patterns in your leadership culture right now.

Called & Burning Out
High Calling + Low Sustainability. Leaders believe deeply in what they're doing — and are paying for it personally. The mission is real. The margin is gone. This is the most urgent pattern to address.
Warm but Guarded
High Belonging + Low Trust & Safety + Low Being Known. Leaders enjoy each other but don't truly know each other. The warmth is genuine. The depth is not yet there. Vulnerability hasn't been normalized.
Empowered but Unheard
High Empowerment + Low Having a Voice. Leaders feel trusted to act but not fully safe to speak. They execute well but hold back honest perspective. The culture gets compliance without full wisdom.
Clear on Mission
High Calling + High Empowerment. Leaders know why they're here, feel trusted, and believe the work matters. This is the strongest asset in your data and the foundation for everything else.
Section 4 — Recommended Path Forward
A Plan to Strengthen Your Culture

These recommendations are drawn directly from your scores, ordered by urgency and impact.

1
Address Sustainability — Now, Not Later
Schedule a leadership team conversation specifically about pace, capacity, and permission to rest. Name the Sustainability score openly. Ask leaders to share what is actually happening for them. This conversation alone — done well — shifts the culture. Create explicit permission for leaders to say they are struggling. Build rest into the rhythm, not as a reward but as a requirement. Without this, everything else is built on a cracking foundation.
2
Build Safety for Honest Conversation
Trust & Safety and Having a Voice are your two most urgent relational scores. The culture needs explicit, repeated signals that honesty is safe — especially disagreement, doubt, and struggle. Consider a quarterly leadership half-day with a simple format: each person shares something real about where they are personally and spiritually. No agenda, no problem-solving. Just honest presence. This slowly normalizes the depth the team is currently missing.
3
Invest in Individual Leader Health
Several leaders are scoring below 50% overall. They are carrying more than the team knows. Consider offering each leader access to individual spiritual direction or pastoral care as part of their role — not as a crisis response, but as standard practice. Leaders who are personally healthy lead from a different place. This is not overhead. It is the soil everything else grows in.
4
Protect What Is Working
Calling and Empowerment are genuine strengths. Name them explicitly in your next team gathering — not as performance praise, but as honest acknowledgment. "We are genuinely called to this. We feel trusted to lead. That is not accidental, and it is worth protecting." Naming what is good anchors the culture and builds the confidence to address what isn't.
5
Create a Support Structure for Leaders
Receiving Support at 57% means leaders feel moderately supported — but not reliably. Build a simple structure: each leader has at least one peer they can call when things are hard. Senior leaders check in personally with each team member at least once a quarter — not about the work, but about the person. Small, consistent practices compound over time into a culture where support is expected, not exceptional.
6
Reassess in 6 Months
Culture shifts are real but slow. Repeat this assessment in six months and compare scores. A 5-point improvement in Sustainability, Trust & Safety, and Having a Voice — sustained over two cycles — represents meaningful, lasting change. The goal is not a perfect score. It is honest, steady movement in the right direction.
Section 5 — What This Makes Possible
Why This Matters

Healthy leaders lead differently. Not just more effectively — differently. They take risks they wouldn't take from a depleted place. They have honest conversations they would otherwise avoid. They model the kind of community they're asking their congregation to build.

The data in this report is not a verdict on your team. It is an invitation. Your leaders care deeply — the Calling and Empowerment scores make that clear. The question is whether the culture around them will sustain that care over the long haul, or slowly drain it.

You now have a picture. You have a starting point. What you do with it is the work — and it is work worth doing.
